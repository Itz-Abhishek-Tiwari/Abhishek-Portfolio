In the realm of Linux desktop customization, few things offer the same sense of control and personalization as window managers and dotfiles. After months of experimenting with various configurations, I’m excited to introduce *NamiConfig*, my own take on a highly customized setup based on Hyprdots. This config merges style, performance, and productivity in one cohesive package, leveraging the power of the Hyprland window manager and a slew of other tools that make the Linux experience both beautiful and functional.

## Why NamiConfig?

At its core, NamiConfig is a refined version of the excellent [Hyprdots configuration by Prasanth Rangan](https://github.com/prasanthrangan/hyprdots), which I’ve adapted to my personal workflow. The vision behind this configuration was to create a polished, lightweight environment that strikes a perfect balance between aesthetics and efficiency. I wanted it to look stunning while being fast and highly responsive, ideal for developers, designers, or anyone who thrives on a minimal yet powerful desktop environment.

### Core Tools in NamiConfig

1. **Hyprland** – As the centerpiece, Hyprland offers a dynamic way to manage windows and workspaces. It’s a wayland-based compositor that is both modern and lightweight, with a focus on flexibility and speed.
   
2. **Kvantum** – For those who crave a consistent and customizable Qt theme, Kvantum is an absolute must. I’ve integrated it deeply into NamiConfig, ensuring that all Qt-based applications follow the overall design theme.

3. **Kitty** – My terminal of choice. Fast, feature-packed, and aesthetically pleasing, Kitty pairs perfectly with the rest of the setup, offering GPU acceleration and smooth font rendering.

4. **Fastfetch** – A lightweight tool for displaying system information on startup. It gives that quick snapshot of the system’s status with a minimal impact on performance.

5. **Waybar** – The customizable status bar I’m using in NamiConfig. It's configured to display all the necessary information—workspaces, system stats, date and time—while blending perfectly into the desktop’s aesthetic.

6. **Rofi** – A powerful launcher, Rofi is used in NamiConfig for application launching, window switching, and more. It’s simple but incredibly versatile when configured properly.

7. **Btop** – For resource monitoring, I’ve opted for Btop due to its visually appealing interface and detailed system information. It’s great for quickly checking CPU, RAM, and network usage without feeling clunky.

8. **LazyGit** – A terminal-based Git client, LazyGit makes it easy to manage repositories, making it a crucial part of my workflow as a developer.

9. **Spicetify** – NamiConfig even extends to music. Using Spicetify, I’ve customized Spotify’s interface to match the Kanagawa color scheme, ensuring that everything remains cohesive, even during a coding session with background music.

10. **Neovim (NvChad)** – NamiConfig includes extensive customizations for Neovim (NvChad), primarily for Python and web development, including Django and React workflows. With `nvim-dap` for debugging, it’s an all-in-one powerhouse for coding.

### Aesthetic: The Kanagawa Colorscheme

A significant part of NamiConfig’s appeal comes from the Kanagawa colorscheme, which I’ve applied across the entire setup. Inspired by traditional Japanese art, this color palette brings warmth and harmony to the desktop. It’s soft on the eyes while providing excellent contrast, perfect for long coding or writing sessions. I’ve made sure that every app, from the terminal to Spotify, stays in sync with this theme, creating a truly unified look.

### Key Features and Customizations

- **Performance Optimizations**: NamiConfig is designed to be lightweight, with optimizations for speed. Hyprland’s compositing is highly responsive, and paired with tools like Kitty and Fastfetch, the overall experience remains snappy even under heavy workloads.

- **Modular Configuration**: NamiConfig is modular, making it easy to modify or extend. Whether you want to change the window management behavior, tweak the bar, or add new functionality, each component can be adapted to suit your needs.

- **Developer-Centric**: The configuration has been tailored with developers in mind, especially those working in Python, Django, and JavaScript (React). With integrated support for `nvim-dap` and enhanced Git management through LazyGit, it's a powerful environment for programming.

- **Music and Media Integration**: With Spicetify and Rofi, managing music and media becomes seamless. Whether you’re switching between coding and relaxing or multitasking between projects, it feels effortless.

### The Future of NamiConfig

NamiConfig is always evolving. I’m constantly learning and refining it to incorporate new tools, better performance enhancements, and, of course, a deeper level of personalization. The ultimate goal is to share it publicly, so that others in the Linux and developer community can benefit from a configuration that emphasizes both beauty and function.

For now, it’s become my daily driver—a workspace that not only looks good but also supercharges productivity. With plans to keep improving it as I explore more tools and configurations, NamiConfig will continue to grow into a more versatile environment.
